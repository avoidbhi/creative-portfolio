A spec sales page — then the teardown.

Vanilla Noir was written as a fictional luxury product: a fragrance that evolves on the skin for 72 hours. The page was built to be perfect — and then annotated, section by section, as a business case study.

How the page is built

  • The headline“The Forbidden Orchid Elixir That Evolves on Your Skin for 72 Hours” — Forbidden (exclusivity) · Orchid Elixir (category lifted from commodity) · Evolves (ongoing value) · 72 Hours (quantified benefit).
  • ScarcityCountdown timer, “Only 9 Bottles Left”, “Batch #7 sold out in 19 minutes. Next harvest: 2026.”
  • NESBNew: “412-day smoking process…” · Easy: “It literally rewrites your brain chemistry” · Safe: “Wear it. Crave it. Or we’ll bottle your tears and refund €200” · Big: “78% Nostalgia Recall · 54% More Head-Turns”.
  • The 4 U’sUrgent + Unique at the top, Useful in the middle, Ultra-Specific at the bottom — “412-day cold-smoking in ex-whiskey barrels”, “250+ aromatic compounds (vs. 8 in synthetic vanillin)”.
  • The storyThe Extraction Report — “the only document in the world” — with a named authority: Dr. Elena Marchetti, Lead Perfumer.

The case study runs ten pages: hero strategy, revenue-driving headline breakdown, scarcity psychology, social-proof structure, and the authority-building percentages behind each technique. The product is imaginary. The framework is the deliverable.

Spec project · written as a portfolio piece, annotated as a teaching one.
